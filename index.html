Important information about your results ▼
  • The calculations given in this tool are to be used as a rough guide only. The actual tax you pay will depend on your personal circumstances and HMRC tax rules which may change in the future.
  • We have assumed a Personal Allowance of £12,570 for the 2025/26 tax year based on the information you have given us. However, your actual personal allowance will depend on your personal circumstances.
  • The tax calculations don't take into account any National Insurance contributions due on non-pension earnings.
  • We assume the tax-free lump sum amount is within your available lump sum allowance.
  • We have assumed that any pension withdrawal amounts referred to are from defined contribution pensions.
Important considerations for pension withdrawals ▼
  • Once you take money from your pension pot, you can't put it back. Consider carefully whether you need the money now or whether it could continue to grow for the future.
  • Taking a large lump sum could push you into a higher tax band for that tax year, meaning you pay more tax overall.
  • If your total income exceeds £100,000, your Personal Allowance will be reduced by £1 for every £2 of income above this limit. This can create an effective tax rate of 60%.
  • Withdrawing pension money may affect your entitlement to means-tested state benefits.
  • Once you flexibly access your pension (beyond the tax-free portion), your annual allowance for future pension contributions may be reduced to £10,000 (the Money Purchase Annual Allowance).
  • Consider spreading withdrawals across multiple tax years to minimise the tax you pay.
Other tax considerations ▼
  • National Insurance contributions are not charged on pension income.
  • If you live in Scotland, different income tax rates and bands apply. This calculator uses rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland only.
  • Savings and dividend income have their own allowances and tax rates which are not included in this calculator.
  • If you are receiving the State Pension, this counts as taxable income and should be included in your "other taxable income" figure.
  • If your pension provider deducts tax at an emergency rate, you may be able to reclaim overpaid tax from HMRC using form P50Z or P55, or through your self-assessment tax return.
